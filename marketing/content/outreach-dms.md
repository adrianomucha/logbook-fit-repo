# Direct Outreach: Instagram DMs and Follow-ups

The highest-converting channel we have, and the least scalable. That tradeoff is
correct at 50 coaches.

## Rules

1. **Never open with the product.** The first message asks a question about their
   business. If it reads like a pitch, it gets deleted in under a second.
2. **Reference something real from their profile.** One specific detail proves a
   human wrote it. This is the entire difference between a 3% and a 20% reply rate.
3. **One question per message.** Two questions is a survey. Nobody answers surveys.
4. **No links in the first message.** Instagram suppresses them and it reads as spam.
5. **If they say no, say thanks and leave.** The fitness coaching space is small
   and people talk. A gracious no is a warm lead in nine months.

## Qualification checklist

Before you send, confirm:

- [ ] Bio indicates they coach individuals (not just sell templates or programs)
- [ ] Posted within the last 30 days
- [ ] No "team", "agency", or multiple coaches listed
- [ ] Not obviously enterprise or a studio with a front desk
- [ ] Roughly 1k to 50k followers (big enough to be real, small enough to reply)

## Opener variants

Send one variant for a week, measure, then switch. Track reply rate per variant.

### Variant A: the retention question (default)

> Hey [Name], saw your post about [specific thing, e.g. the 12-week program you
> just wrapped]. Random question since you actually coach people 1:1: when a
> client starts to fade, do you usually catch it early, or do you find out when
> the renewal doesn't come through?

**Why it works:** It's a real question with an uncomfortable answer, and it
signals you understand the job. Most coaches have a story here and want to tell it.

### Variant B: the research angle

> Hey [Name], I'm building a tool for independent coaches and I'm trying not to
> build the wrong thing. Can I ask you one question? What's the part of running
> your coaching that you'd hand off tomorrow if you could?

**Why it works:** "Help me not screw this up" is a much easier ask than "buy my
thing." Coaches who teach for a living are wired to answer.

### Variant C: the specific pain (for coaches who've posted about admin)

> Hey [Name], you mentioned in [post/story] that the admin side is eating your
> week. How are you tracking check-ins right now? I keep hearing spreadsheet plus
> WhatsApp and I'm trying to work out if that's actually the norm.

**Why it works:** It quotes them back to themselves. Highest reply rate of the
three when the trigger post exists, but it only applies to a subset.

## Second message, after they reply

Do not pitch yet. Go one layer deeper first. Coaches who answer two questions
convert far better than coaches who answer one, because they've now invested.

> That tracks with basically everyone I've talked to. Follow-up: when you lost
> your last client, how long were they quiet before you noticed?

Then, once they've given you the second answer:

> That's the exact thing I'm building for. Short version: it's a coaching
> platform that sorts your client list by who's about to fade instead of
> alphabetically. Quiet client hits the top of the dashboard before the refund
> email hits your inbox.
>
> It's in private beta, free while we're building. Want me to send you a spot?
> Genuinely fine if not, this was useful either way.

**The "genuinely fine if not" line matters.** It removes the pressure that makes
people ghost, and it makes the yes mean something.

## Handling the common replies

**"I already use Trainerize / TrueCoach."**

> Makes sense, both are solid at the plan-building part. Are you happy with what
> they tell you about *who needs you this week*? That's the only bit I'm trying
> to do better. Most coaches I talk to say the dashboard shows them everyone
> equally, which is the same as showing them nothing.

(If they're on TrueCoach, the 5% card transaction fee introduced in January 2026
is a live wound. Don't lead with it, but if pricing comes up, it's fair game.)

**"How much is it?"**

> Free through the beta. I'm not taking payment until it's genuinely saving you
> a client, and I'd rather have your feedback than your money right now.

**"Send me the link."**

> logbook.fit, the waitlist form is right on the homepage. Put a note in when you
> sign up saying we spoke and I'll move you into the next batch.

(A named referral to yourself creates a small commitment and lets you match the
waitlist entry to the conversation.)

**"Sounds like every other app."**

> Fair, there are a lot of them. The difference is what the home screen is for.
> Every other one opens on your full client list. This one opens on the three
> people who've gone quiet. If that's not a problem you have, it's genuinely not
> for you.

Do not fight this objection past one message. Conceding gracefully is more
persuasive than the counter-argument.

---

## Follow-up, once, after 5 to 7 days of silence

Only if they opened but never replied. Never twice.

> No worries if this isn't a priority, just closing the loop: still happy to hold
> a beta spot for you if you want one. Either way, good luck with [thing they
> posted about].

Then stop. One follow-up is diligence, two is harassment.

## Email version, for coaches with a public business address

Subject lines to test:

- `quick question about client churn`
- `how long before you notice a client's gone quiet?`
- `[Name], one question about your coaching admin`

Body:

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'll keep this short. I'm building a tool for independent coaches and I'm
> talking to as many as I can before I build more of the wrong thing.
>
> One question: when a client starts to fade, do you usually catch it early, or
> do you find out when they don't renew?
>
> I ask because the stat that got me started on this is that around 60% of
> clients who actually hit their goal still leave, mostly because nothing came
> after the win. That seemed fixable.
>
> No pitch attached. If you've got 30 seconds to reply I'd genuinely appreciate it.
>
> [Your name]
> logbook.fit

## Tracking

Keep one sheet. Columns:

`handle | source | date sent | variant | replied? | second reply? | outcome | notes`

Review weekly. If a variant is under 10% reply after 40 sends, kill it and write
a new one. The notes column is where your best future content comes from: real
coaches describing real pain in their own words is better copy than anything you
will invent at a desk.
