# SEO Content Plan

Slow channel, start now. Two categories: **high-intent comparison pages** that
capture coaches actively shopping, and **problem-aware guides** that capture
coaches who haven't realized software is the answer yet.

Suggested location: `/blog` on logbook.fit. These do not need to be pretty, they
need to exist and be honest.

---

## Priority 1: the comparison pages

There is a genuinely good window open right now, and it will close.

- **TrueCoach began charging a 5% transaction fee on all credit card payments in
  January 2026**, on top of the subscription. A coach billing $4,000/month is now
  paying an extra $200/month. Those coaches are searching for alternatives today.
- **Trainerize** has been losing coaches since the ABC Fitness acquisition, with
  complaints about bugs, program delivery failures, sync issues, and pricing that
  scales badly.

The pages currently ranking for these terms are mostly competitor-written ads
wearing a comparison costume. An honest page written by someone who understands
the workflow can beat them, and honesty is also the only version that converts,
because a coach who arrives feeling misled bounces.

### Article A: "TrueCoach Alternatives in 2026: What to Check Before You Switch"

**Target:** `truecoach alternatives`, `truecoach 5% fee`, `truecoach transaction fee`

**Angle:** Lead with the fee math, because that's what brought them. Then widen
into what actually matters when switching.

**Outline:**
1. **The fee, in real numbers.** A table: at $2k, $4k, $8k monthly billing, here's
   what 5% costs you per year on top of subscription. Do not editorialize, the
   arithmetic is enough.
2. **When switching isn't worth it.** Genuinely: if you're under 8 clients, or
   you bill outside the platform anyway, the fee may not touch you. Say so. This
   paragraph is what makes the rest credible.
3. **What to actually evaluate.** Payment cut, price at 10 vs 30 clients, client
   app quality, data export (can you leave?), and the one nobody lists: does it
   tell you who needs attention, or just show you a list?
4. **The options.** Everfit, Trainerize, QuickCoach, Logbook.fit. Honest one-liners
   on who each is genuinely best for. Name a competitor as the better choice for
   at least one use case, because it's true and because it's the most persuasive
   sentence on the page.
5. **Where Logbook.fit fits.** Solo coach, 5 to 40 clients, retention is the
   problem. Not for you if you need meal planning or run a team.

### Article B: "The Best Trainerize Alternative for Solo Coaches"

**Target:** `trainerize alternative`, `trainerize alternative solo coach`, `trainerize too expensive`

**Angle:** Trainerize is built for businesses with staff. Most of its surface area
is overhead if you're one person. That's not a flaw in Trainerize, it's a
mismatch, and framing it that way is both fairer and more convincing than
trash-talk.

**Outline:**
1. Why solo coaches outgrow it in the wrong direction (paying for team features,
   fighting a UI built for gyms).
2. What post-acquisition churn actually looks like, sourced and dated, no rumor.
3. The switching checklist, including data export.
4. Options table, same honesty rules as Article A.
5. Who Logbook is for.

## Priority 2: the problem-aware guides

These target coaches who know they have a churn problem but don't know a tool
exists. Bigger long-term traffic, longer to rank, and they feed the newsletter.

### Article C: "Why Personal Training Clients Quit (And the 3 Weeks Where You Can Still Save Them)"

**Target:** `why do personal training clients quit`, `personal trainer client retention`

This is the flagship. Longest, most linkable, and the source of most social
repurposing.

**Outline:**
1. The numbers: 3 to 6 month average relationship, up to 50% annual churn, and
   the 60% of goal-achievers who leave anyway.
2. The three-week decay pattern (busy → embarrassed → decided). This is the
   original framework in the piece and the reason people will link to it.
3. Why the goal-achievement moment is the highest-churn moment in coaching.
4. Why silence is invisible to every system you own. Spreadsheets record what you
   typed. Chat apps show what was said. Neither surfaces an absence.
5. A practical weekly triage routine that works with no software at all. Give
   this away properly, with the actual steps.
6. When a spreadsheet stops being enough (roughly 8 to 10 clients), and what to
   look for in a tool.

### Article D: "The Post-Goal Churn Trap: What to Do the Week a Client Wins"

**Target:** `client finished their program what next`, `personal training client achieved goal now what`

Low volume, extremely high intent, almost nobody has written it well. Also the
best candidate for a PTDC guest pitch, since the 60% stat is theirs.

**Outline:** the celebration-then-silence pattern, why "let's take a break and
regroup" is a cancellation in disguise, a script for the conversation to have two
weeks *before* the goal date, and how to structure the next block so it exists
before it's needed.

### Article E: "Client Management for Personal Trainers: Spreadsheet, Chat, or Software?"

**Target:** `personal trainer client management`, `personal trainer spreadsheet template`

**The trick:** actually give away a good spreadsheet template. It's the best
link-bait and email-capture asset in this list, it's genuinely useful, and it
sells the software better than a landing page does, because everyone who uses it
eventually hits the ceiling it can't cross.

## Priority 3: quick wins

Short pages, cheap to write, decent long-tail:

- "How many clients can one online coach actually handle?"
- "What should a coaching check-in actually ask?" (publish the exact questions
  used in the product)
- "Personal trainer client retention rate: what's normal?" (the stats page,
  properly sourced, becomes a citation target)

---

## Rules for all of it

1. **Never write a comparison you'd be embarrassed for a competitor to read.**
   The category is full of dishonest comparison pages and coaches are sick of
   them. Being the honest one is a positioning asset, not a handicap.
2. **Date and source every stat.** Link the primary source. It's the difference
   between a citation target and content slop.
3. **Recommend a competitor at least once per comparison page**, where it's
   genuinely true. It costs you a small number of poorly-fitting signups and buys
   you every well-fitting one.
4. **One clear CTA per page**, to the waitlist. Not three.
5. **No em dashes** in published copy, per house style.

## Realistic expectations

Nothing here ranks in month one. The comparison pages should start moving in
6 to 10 weeks, the guides in 3 to 6 months. Write them now precisely because
they're slow, and do not let them eat time that belongs to DMs and Reddit.
